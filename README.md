## Direction 1: The Ration Book

### Concept

In this direction, I bring the three articles together as a publication that emphasizes everyday use rather than individual authorship.

I interpret these articles as different challenges to mainstream design history: Wilkins critiques narratives centered on heroic designers; Toppins questions the organization of design history along a single timeline; and Lupton and Xia challenge the treatment of a particular style as a neutral, universal standard. Together, they prompted me to focus on designs that serve everyday life without foregrounding the designer’s reputation. The wartime ration book mentioned in *No More Heroes* inspired me and reminded me of China’s grain coupons and ration tickets from other countries. These printed materials derive their value from practical use: they are collected, marked, torn off, and eventually discarded.

I plan to use the ration book as a shared framework for the collection, with each article borrowing typography and information hierarchy from a different type of ticket. I will explore how three actions crossing out, tearing off, and notching corners can translate into visual markers and rhythms within the reading experience. These references will connect the three articles through a consistent visual language.

### Design

The color scheme for the first article was derived from the 1966 nationwide grain ration coupons used in China—the most common and widely circulated ration tickets of the time. Once used, these coupons were stamped or collected, signifying that they were no longer valid.


## Direction 2: The Critical Reader

### Concept
Each article challenges certain notions that are often taken for granted: the use of timelines as a way to organize history, the portrayal of individual designers as central figures in history, and the idea that “graphic design can remain neutral.” Together, these articles demonstrate that the way we organize and present information influences what readers focus on and how they interpret it. Through typographic design, I hope to make these issues feel personally relevant and worthy of discussion.

I drew inspiration from the Women Workshop website (https://seeredwomensworkshop.com/) and plan to combine bold sans-serif headings with serif body text and expressive italicized quotations. Drawing inspiration from the direct style of protest posters and independent publications, I will explore strong color contrasts and variations in type size to give this collection of articles a clear and powerful voice. Throughout these three articles, these design choices will follow a consistent hierarchy. The goal is to make the arguments visually engaging while ensuring a clear and comfortable reading experience.
